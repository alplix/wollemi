## Disposal from 700 km (IADC guideline: 25 years)

- Passive with wings only: 45-50 years: **fails**.
- Passive with a 1 m2 sail: 14-20 years: meets 25 years on this model, but the uncertainty (factor 2-3) could push it above 25; treat the sail as the passive backup, not the plan.
- **Propulsive descent** with the electric thruster: 700 -> 600 km costs 54 m/s (~9 days of thrusting at 1.1 mN, then 2-8 years of drag with the sail); 700 -> 500 km costs 108 m/s (~19 days, then 0.9-3.9 years). A perigee-lowering burn to 300 km costs 110 m/s (~19 days).
- Delta-v plan at 700 km: 39 m/s station keeping x1.5 margin (59) + 108 m/s descent + 20 m/s collision avoidance = **187 m/s of 567 m/s available** (9500 Ns class thruster on 16.8 kg; `docs/propulsion.md`).
- 800 km and above would need propulsion for disposal (passive decay takes decades to centuries) and are not recommended without a disposal plan.

## Consequences for the design

- The drag sail is required, not optional (also as the passive backup if propulsion fails at end of life); the propulsion bay keeps propellant reserved for the descent (about 108 m/s).
- 600 km is technically possible for 50 years (175 m/s) but leaves a smaller margin against collision-avoidance needs; 700 km balances lifetime and disposal.
- Collision risk with catalogued and uncatalogued debris is **not evaluated**: run ESA MASTER / NASA ORDEM for the chosen orbit and use the conjunction data messages (`mission/requirements.md`, COLAV).
