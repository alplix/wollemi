Mission and objectives

Wollemi is an open, modular, long-life 12U science observatory and reference platform. It produces multi-decade space-weather, radiation and Earth-observation data, demonstrates robust electronics and software in orbit, and publishes the design, data and tools for anyone to reuse.

OBJ-1

Space-weather & geophysics

Long-record data: magnetic field, energetic particles, solar X-ray/UV, total solar irradiance, radiation dose, plasma, VLF, gamma-ray bursts.

OBJ-2

Earth observation

5 m class imaging telescope, hyperspectral scenes sharing the same optics, thermal infrared, wide-field night imagery.

OBJ-3

Technology & reliability science

Radiation behaviour of three different compute families and of flash memory types over years, with the error data published openly.

OBJ-4

A reusable platform

The bus, cell grid, backplane, protocol and tooling are designed once and reused: a new mission is a configuration change plus new cards, not a redesign.

OBJ-5

Fully open, on release

Hardware under CERN-OHL-S-2.0, software under Apache-2.0, documents under CC-BY-4.0 -- once the design is complete and a trademark check is finished.

Long-life by design

A tiered survival architecture: the beacon and supervisor are designed to outlive the batteries, which are designed to outlive the heavy compute -- graceful degradation, not a single point of failure.

In orbit

The spacecraft is the real CAD geometry (`mechanical/wollemi_cad.py`), rendered with a real path tracer (Blender/Cycles) using an actual NASA Blue Marble Earth photograph; the solar-cell and MLI-foil surface materials are illustrative, and the starfield is procedural, but the lighting and geometry are physically simulated, not hand-drawn.

Deployment sequence

A schematic illustration of the wings unfolding from the stowed launch configuration to fully deployed. The hinge/accordion mechanism itself is not yet designed (see the open items below), so this animation interpolates each panel's stowed and deployed footprint rather than simulating a real mechanism -- it exists to communicate the concept, not to validate it.

Two wings, three panels each

Each wing carries three double-sided panels (cells on both faces, needed because the preferred dawn-dusk orbit keeps the sun on the wing-normal axis rather than always on one side). Six hold-down posts per stack keep the launch-configuration vibration mode comfortably above the rideshare minimum.

At a glance

Form12U CubeSat envelope, 2×2×3 cell grid around a central spine, notched 100×100 mm cards
Mass / powerabout 16.8 of 24 kg budget; 58 W sun-biased, 28 W tumbling, 3 W safe-mode load
Science payload~26 instruments: 5 m class telescope + hyperspectral (shared optics), thermal IR, magnetometer, particle spectrometer, X-ray/UV monitor, total solar irradiance, VLF/ELF, gamma-ray burst counter, radiation dosimetry, SEU/flash experiments
ComputeMSP430FR supervisor, 2×STM32H7 flight controllers, mass memory unit (8×128 GB, 6+2 erasure coding), Raspberry Pi CM5, NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano
Powertwo independent LiFePO4 chains (A/B) plus a fully independent survival chain, hardware-only protection (no MCU in the trip path)
Propulsioniodine electric gridded-ion thruster on the centre line, canted through the centre of mass; ~570 m/s available delta-v, drag sail as passive backup
CommsUHF/LoRa beacon and commanding, S-band science downlink (250 kbps–2 Mbps adaptive), CCSDS Space Packets with Ed25519-signed telecommands
Orbit (preferred)dawn-dusk sun-synchronous, about 700 km -- removes eclipse cycling, favoured for thermal and battery-life reasons
Verification47 requirements traced; most checked automatically on every run; 29 planned physical tests

Done at analysis level

  • Requirements, architecture, parametric CAD with interference checks
  • Power, thermal, attitude, orbit and structural analyses
  • Full command/telemetry protocol (27 messages) with C, Python and Kaitai Struct implementations
  • Firmware core (authentication, mode manager, fault detection, OTA) with 84 unit checks
  • Electronics interface: KiCad card and backplane templates, EPS/survival card floor plans, DRC clean
  • FMEA, verification matrix, test plan, operations concept, cost and schedule model

Still open

  • No hardware exists: vendor data, real schematics, prototypes and physical tests are all ahead
  • Launch opportunity, operator of record and frequency coordination not yet arranged
  • Wing hinge/deployment mechanism not yet designed (this site's deployment GIF is illustrative only)
  • Double-sided solar panels have no found commercial precedent -- a custom part
  • Choice of target still open: flight mission, flight-quality reference design, or a FlatSat/balloon precursor first
Read this plainly: every number on this page is an engineering estimate from a simulation or a hand calculation, not a measurement. No part of Wollemi has been built, and nothing here is qualified for flight.
