Mission and objectives

Wollemi is an open, modular, long-life 12U science observatory and reference platform. It produces multi-decade space-weather, radiation and Earth-observation data, demonstrates robust electronics and software in orbit, and publishes the design, data and tools for anyone to reuse.

OBJ-1

Space-weather & geophysics

Long-record data: magnetic field, energetic particles, solar X-ray/UV, total solar irradiance, radiation dose, plasma, VLF, gamma-ray bursts.

OBJ-2

Earth observation

5 m class imaging telescope, hyperspectral scenes sharing the same optics, thermal infrared, wide-field night imagery.

OBJ-3

Technology & reliability science

Radiation behaviour of three different compute families and of flash memory types over years, with the error data published openly.

OBJ-4

A reusable platform

The bus, cell grid, backplane, protocol and tooling are designed once and reused: a new mission is a configuration change plus new cards, not a redesign.

OBJ-5

Fully open, now

Hardware under CERN-OHL-S-2.0, software under Apache-2.0, documents under CC-BY-4.0 -- published and REUSE-compliant alongside the rest of the design.

Long-life by design

A tiered survival architecture: the beacon and supervisor are designed to outlive the batteries, which are designed to outlive the heavy compute -- graceful degradation, not a single point of failure.

Deployment sequence

A schematic illustration of the wings unfolding from the stowed launch configuration to fully deployed. The hinge/accordion mechanism itself is not yet designed (see the open items below), so this animation interpolates each panel's stowed and deployed footprint rather than simulating a real mechanism -- it exists to communicate the concept, not to validate it.

Two wings, three panels each

Each wing carries three double-sided panels (cells on both faces, needed because the preferred dawn-dusk orbit keeps the sun on the wing-normal axis rather than always on one side). Six hold-down posts per stack keep the launch-configuration vibration mode comfortably above the rideshare minimum.

Electronics & PCB design

The spacecraft is built as a card cage: identical 100×100 mm cards slide into a passive backplane, one column per instrument/subsystem group. Every board below is a real KiCad file in the repo ( electronics/kicad/), DRC-clean, generated from the same card_pinout.toml connector definition the interface document is built from -- these are floor plans (component placement proven to fit), not yet routed schematics.

Every card shares one connector pair: WL-P (2×30, power and control -- dual battery-bus rails, redundant CAN-FD, PPS/SYNC timing, a hardware KILL_N line the supervisor can assert with no software in the path, and an SWD header so any card can be reprogrammed without removing it) and WL-D (2×15, Ethernet/high-speed lanes, populated only on data-plane cards). Connector pitch is 1.27 mm on purpose -- an earlier 0.8 mm pitch could not be routed on the backplane without the power chains blocking each other.

Subsystems

Every subsystem is generated from the same configuration files the checks run against, so a design change and its documentation can never quietly drift apart.

SubsystemArchitecture
StructureAn aluminium frame built around a 40 mm central spine in a 2×2×3 cell grid (226.3×226.3×340.5 mm envelope). Every card in the spacecraft shares one 100×100 mm outline with a 20×20 mm spine notch and an 8 mm rail relief -- a format invented for this project because standard 90/100 mm boards don't clear the spine. Six hold-down posts per wing stack, not just the corners, push the stowed first mode to about 137 Hz; an earlier corners-only analysis found only 99 Hz.
PowerTwo independent MPPT/battery chains (A, B) plus a fully independent survival chain (C) with its own dedicated body-mounted solar string; per-card eFuse and a hardware KILL_N line, with no software anywhere in the protection path.
ThermalFully passive. Analysis found the spacecraft runs cold rather than hot -- the real fight is a -27 °C eclipse case and a -40 °C safe-mode case, held off by splitting roughly half the surface between the solar cells and dedicated MLI/low-e patches. The telescope column gets its own low-α/low-ε coating and an 8 W thermostatic heater (14-17 °C setpoint); the battery vault is isolated (0.1 W/K) with a 6 W heater, and the survival string has its own 2 W heater so it never drops below -20 °C.
Attitude & propulsionThree reaction wheels (30 mN·m·s each), magnetorquers and a star tracker (5 arcsec, ~17 m ground error) close a 90° slew in about 120 s with 3x+ margin on torque and momentum. Propulsion is electric (iodine gridded-ion/Hall-class) rather than a water resistojet for the specific impulse: 0.5 kg of iodine buys ~570 m/s against ~45 m/s from 0.8 kg of water -- enough for 50-year drag makeup, collision avoidance and a controlled deorbit from one tank. The thruster sits on the centre line, canted a few degrees through the centre of mass; an earlier in-column placement produced enough off-axis torque to saturate a wheel in seven minutes. A drag sail is the passive backup.
ComputeA five-tier hierarchy, deliberately over-provisioned at the bottom: an MSP430FR supervisor and dual hot-standby STM32H7 flight controllers are rated for the full 50-year mission, because the long-record space-weather science has to keep running long after the Linux side is expected to have failed. A Raspberry Pi CM5 (always on) and an NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano (imaging, heavy compute) sit above that with 10-15 year design lives. The mass memory unit holds 8×128 GB with 6+2 erasure coding, BLAKE2 hashing and periodic scrubbing.
Data & comms27 CCSDS message types, every telecommand Ed25519-signed with a counter and validity window (the link itself is unencrypted, as amateur-band rules require). The control plane is dual CAN-FD (1 Mbps/5 Mbps) for redundancy; a gigabit Ethernet plane carries payload data. Downlink is UHF/LoRa housekeeping (~0.5 MB/day) plus an adaptive S-band science link (250 kbps-2 Mbps) -- from the Pamukkale reference station alone, roughly 28 minutes of contact and ~134 MB/day across 3-4 passes.
Payload26 instruments split cleanly by lifetime. Long-record space-weather/geophysics science (three-axis magnetometer, total-solar-irradiance radiometer, radiation dosimeter, a 16-electron/16-proton particle spectrometer, an X-ray/UV monitor, a CsI+SiPM gamma-ray-burst detector, a chip-scale atomic clock) is acquired directly by the flight controllers, never the Linux nodes. Imaging -- an 85 mm Cassegrain telescope at roughly 5 m ground sample distance, a ~50-band hyperspectral channel sharing the same optics, a thermal-IR microbolometer and a wide-field star/context camera -- runs on the Pi/Jetson side. Every instrument number above is a design target verified so far only by analysis, not by a built and tested instrument.
Ground & operationsTwo published ground-segment tiers: a $30-80 UHF/LoRa listener anyone can build to receive the beacon, and a ~$2,200-4,500 S-band science station (1.2 m dish, SPID BIG-RAS rotator) for the full downlink. All 27 protocol messages already have a compiled, verified Kaitai Struct decoder, with SatNOGS DB/Decoder integration planned so any station can decode the telemetry without touching this repo. A second anchor ground station is proposed but not yet arranged, and frequency coordination/licensing hasn't started.

Fifty years is longer than most satellite operators last. The supervisor and beacon are built to still be signalling long after everyone who wrote this code has moved on.

Done at analysis level

  • Requirements, architecture, parametric CAD with interference checks
  • Power, thermal, attitude, orbit and structural analyses
  • Full command/telemetry protocol (27 messages) with C, Python and Kaitai Struct implementations
  • Firmware core (authentication, mode manager, fault detection, OTA) with 84 unit checks
  • Electronics interface: KiCad card and backplane templates, EPS/survival card floor plans, DRC clean
  • FMEA, verification matrix, test plan, operations concept, cost and schedule model

Still open

  • No hardware exists: vendor data, real schematics, prototypes and physical tests are all ahead
  • Launch opportunity, operator of record and frequency coordination not yet arranged
  • Wing hinge/deployment mechanism not yet designed (this site's deployment GIF is illustrative only)
  • Double-sided solar panels have no found commercial precedent -- a custom part
  • Choice of target still open: flight mission, flight-quality reference design, or a FlatSat/balloon precursor first
Read this plainly: every number on this page is an engineering estimate from a simulation or a hand calculation, not a measurement. No part of Wollemi has been built, and nothing here is qualified for flight.
